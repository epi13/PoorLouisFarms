Poor Louis Farms is run by Alexander and Katherine Collamore in Wasilla, Alaska. They moved north from Washington in 2012 and are raising three children alongside the evolving work of the farm.

The name carries a family memory. Alexander's late grandfather, Robert Louis Triebenbach, helped inspire Alexander's dream of becoming a farmer. “Louis” keeps that influence close without turning the past into a polished legend.

## A farm shaped by making

Alexander and Katherine bring design, fabrication, and hands-on backgrounds to the work. That helps explain why Poor Louis Farms does not fit inside one product category.

Tea asks for attention to plant material, drying, and the cup. Spice blends invite proportion and texture. Hot sauce adds fermentation, acidity, heat, and bottling. Salves move through flowers, infusion, oils, and wax. Home goods bring in another kind of making altogether.

The thread is not a narrow specialty. It is curiosity made practical.

## Alaska as working context

Alaska is more than scenery in the brand story. Short, intense growing periods, long summer light, winter planning, local materials, and changing harvest windows all shape what can happen and when.

That does not mean every ingredient is grown on the farm or gathered in Alaska. The site names local origin only where it is confirmed. “Alaskan” can also accurately describe the place where the work, testing, and making happen.

## Still becoming

Poor Louis Farms grows, harvests, creates, and experiments. Some ideas settle into a recurring product. Others may appear for a season, change form, or remain in the notebook. Work by other Alaskan makers may also sit naturally alongside the farm's own creations when the fit is right.

That open-endedness is part of the point. The name honors an influence; the farm carries the dream forward by continuing to make, learn, and revise.
