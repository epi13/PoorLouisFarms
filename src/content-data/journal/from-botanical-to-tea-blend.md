A tea blend does not begin at the scale. It begins with knowing exactly what plant and plant part are in hand.

For Poor Louis Farms fireweed tea, that means leaves—not flowers. For Raspberry Leaf Tea Bags, the published ingredient statement is 100% raspberry leaves. Those simple names hide a series of decisions about condition, cleaning, drying, texture, proportion, and the cup the maker wants to produce.

## Start with a clear identity

Positive botanical identification is non-negotiable. So is permission to gather. Cultivated material brings its own records: seed or plant source, growing conditions, and harvest notes. Wild material adds land rules and the obligation to leave a healthy stand behind.

Once selected, material is sorted. Damaged leaves, unrelated plant matter, soil, and insects need appropriate attention before the batch advances.

## Dry for the intended result

Drying removes moisture, but that plain description understates its influence. Airflow, temperature, humidity, leaf thickness, and batch depth can change color, aroma, texture, and time. A leaf intended to remain recognizable in a loose tea may be handled differently from a material destined for grinding.

This is not a home-preservation formula. Safe, repeatable production depends on controls suited to the material and product. The principle is simply that drying is part of flavor design, not an invisible pause between harvest and packaging.

## Blend by tasting, not decoration

A botanical can look beautiful and still overwhelm a cup. Blending asks how ingredients behave together at brewing strength: Which aroma arrives first? Does one leaf mute another? Does the finish invite another sip?

Even a single-ingredient tea benefits from this mindset because the maker is still choosing particle size, serving amount, and batch consistency.

Small-batch testing makes comparison possible. Notes from one cup inform the next adjustment. The goal is not to erase natural variation, but to understand it well enough to make an honest, enjoyable product.
