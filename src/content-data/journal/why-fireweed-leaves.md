Fireweed earns its name and much of its public attention from a vivid column of flowers. In Alaska, people watch those blooms move upward through the summer. It is easy, then, to assume that anything called “fireweed tea” must be flower tea.

Poor Louis Farms makes its fireweed tea from the leaves.

## Leaves behave like tea material

Leaves offer the structure needed for the farm's two current approaches. They can be carefully dried for a mild, direct herbal infusion. They can also be handled through fermentation and aging to build a darker, fuller profile.

The published ingredient statement for Fireweed Loose Leaf Tea is simply 100% fireweed leaves. The Fireweed Pu'er listing likewise identifies aged fireweed leaves. No flowers are named in either tea.

That does not make the flower unimportant. It is a field marker, a seasonal signal, a source of color in the Alaska landscape, and an ecological part of the whole plant. It simply has a different role from the leaf in these products.

## Timing changes character

UAF Cooperative Extension describes young leaves as tender before flowering and notes that leaf flavor becomes stronger as the plant matures. Poor Louis Farms has not published a rigid harvest calendar, and this site does not invent one. Weather, site, plant condition, and the intended batch all affect a real decision in the field.

The useful idea is broader: ingredient quality begins before a leaf reaches the drying shelf. Plant stage and selection shape what processing can later reveal.

## Precision builds trust

“Made with fireweed” is evocative but incomplete. “Made with fireweed leaves” is better. It lets someone picture the actual material, understand why different processes are possible, and avoid repeating an appealing but inaccurate flower-tea story.

That kind of precision is central to this field guide. When a species, ingredient source, or exact formula is not confirmed, we say so. When a product uses leaves rather than flowers, we say that too.
