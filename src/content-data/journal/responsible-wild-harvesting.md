The visible moment of a wild harvest is a hand meeting a plant. The responsible work begins earlier.

## Know the plant

Positive identification means more than finding a picture that looks right. Use regional keys, multiple characteristics, and knowledgeable local instruction. Learn toxic look-alikes. Observe the plant through more than one stage when possible.

An identification app can help organize questions; it should not be the final authority for something you plan to eat or sell.

## Know the place

Permission depends on ownership, land designation, species, intended use, and sometimes the gatherer's eligibility. Rules on private land, state land, National Forests, and National Parks are not interchangeable. Commercial use can be treated differently from personal gathering.

Check with the landowner or managing agency before collecting. “Public land” is not a universal permission slip.

## Know the stand

Broad stewardship guidance from the USDA Forest Service reflects practices many experienced gatherers recognize: collect where the target plant is plentiful, never take every usable leaf or fruit, rotate harvest areas, minimize trampling, and leave material for plant reproduction and wildlife.

The exact light-touch method depends on the species and part. Taking a few leaves is different from removing a root. Snipping young tree growth is different from gathering fruit. A percentage rule copied from the internet cannot replace understanding how that plant recovers.

## Keep a useful record

Field notes can include date, general area, land permission, weather, plant condition, part gathered, quantity, and what was deliberately left. For a maker, traceability is part of craft.

Poor Louis Farms' public catalog describes fireweed leaves, spruce tips, and rosehips in products. It does not publish exact gathering sites—and this educational map does not reveal or invent them. Respect for a place sometimes includes keeping it unpinned.

The goal is not to turn gathering into a performance of purity. It is to make careful decisions that leave a healthy relationship between plant, place, gatherer, and future season.
